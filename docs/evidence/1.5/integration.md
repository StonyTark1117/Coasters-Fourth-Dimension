## Physical limits and measured workload

[Per-native-step measurements](fd2001-native/physical-limits.md) record velocity,
acceleration, jerk, emergency braking, stopping excursion/time and actual retained
mass/actuator caps for the finite presets. The specification distinguishes analytic
command-coordinate limits from sampled physical COM/spatial derivatives. Native
solver wake/braking transients are retained, not smoothed into a false physical
jerk guarantee. Side-drop's collector did not observe first sleep before explicit
Resume; the record reports that limitation and the actual stopped guard instead
of inventing a stopping time. These are observed bounds, not continuous peaks or
passenger comfort guarantees.

The declared four-car admission and fifth-car refusal pass with actual linked
native cars. [Two-table contention](contention.md) proves exclusive receiving-span
ownership and atomic losing admission. The separate maximum workload uses a
48-block bed with four ordinary native cars, a full 360-degree show plus 90-degree
destination selection, emergency stop/Resume, 2,868 native samples and 151 native
mass units. Its final maximum car transform error is 0.0002515934 m.

Maximum-load addon callbacks average **388,097 ns**; whole-server ticks average
**7.008967 ms**, with a **3,124.662182 ms maximum** including synchronous
capture/Move geometry validation. No outlier was removed. This accelerated
headless run is not a real-time park, client FPS or smoothness qualification.
Synchronous validation can cause a visible pause at this capacity.

The existing [FD904 budgets](../../PERFORMANCE.md) explicitly apply to their
host and three fixed-track benchmark scenes. They remain 120,000 ns/invocation
and 6,000 ns/carrier; the new maximum moving workload exceeds the former value
and is not reported as a pass against it. FD2007 requires measured maximum admitted
workload, not that this different scene inherit the fixed benchmark's budget.
No threshold is raised, earlier scene is relabeled, or maximum-capacity smoothness
promise is added. This closes the finite measurement requirement with the disclosed
cost and preserves accepted fixed-workload qualification.
