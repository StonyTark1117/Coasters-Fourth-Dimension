# FD401 coupled Arrow train with Engineered operator controls

This packaged NeoForge client used `-PfdPackaged -PfdValidation -PfdIntegrations=engineered -PfdFeNetwork` with `requireEnergy=true` on the continued `FD401FamilyBare` world. The validation-only `/fd validation engineered_loop` command created an empty original Arrow circuit at `(800,142,12)` with no native chain drive or allocated car. The player fitted the Engineered motor by its normal item to the first straight's start anchor `(800,142,20)`; the [span query](fd401-arrow-train-span.png) records `T0=0`, `T1=0.9583333431579103`. A command supplied 100,000 FE directly to the motor as a disclosed family-test input. The separate wired water-wheel run covers physical FE delivery.

The player placed two Arrow cars by normal item right click at `z=26.5` and `z=32.5`. With a Minecraft Chain, the player selected one cart and clicked the other. The [raw log](client.log) records `linked carts 8c3c2c3a-f035-47f6-99f9-bd67600a6c20 <-> 9e7ce065-efcd-4475-a715-7fdfdde5c574 at distance 6.000`. The player bound the existing station at `(794,142,32)` by empty-hand right click; its [editor](fd401-arrow-train-station-bound.png) reported `arrow_controlled | Ready | Restraints open`. A normal controller item was linked to the motor and placed at `(794,142,22)`. Its GUI was set to `Launch 8 → Stop at 8 → End`, confirmed by the [block query](fd401-arrow-train-controller-nbt.png).

The player boarded an actual Arrow seat on the second car, closed restraints through the in-ride editor, pressed **Dispatch**, and powered the controller by adjacent redstone. During the ride the controller advanced to STOP, its `PassReached` reached `7.958256244659484/8` with `PassShort=0`, and FE fell below the initial 100,000. Rider queries show movement from the station straight through `z≈55`, around the opposite side at `x≈817,z≈15.5`, and back near the station. The train slowed under the programmed STOP within station range. Powering the station's rear input at `(794,142,33)` captured it; the [in-ride editor](fd401-arrow-train-capture.png) returned `Ready | Restraints closed`. The player opened restraints, Shift-dismounted, removed temporary redstone, and [availability](fd401-arrow-train-unload.png) returned `assignments=0`.

The player temporarily set Creative flight/gravity to hold the item-placement camera at track height, then restored gravity before quitting. This is an ordinary item/link/seat/controller interaction run on a validation-built route, not a survival acquisition or physical energy-generation run. It demonstrates one coupled Arrow circuit and operator capture/unload; it does not claim exact launch-speed attainment or repeated reliability.

The client [log](client.log) ends with `All dimensions are saved` and Gradle `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`; the [stopped world](world.tar.gz) preserves the coupled train. The [SHA-256 inventory](evidence.sha256) covers retained payloads. Shared Gradle output was used during concurrent work, so the post-run JAR file hash alone does not establish the exact in-use runtime JAR identity.
