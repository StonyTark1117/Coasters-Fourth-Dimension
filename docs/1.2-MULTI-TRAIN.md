# Ride-local multi-train operation (1.2)

This guide covers the bounded `arrow_fixed_circuit_v1` example. It is one fixed
eight-segment Arrow circuit divided into six route blocks. It supports at most
three simultaneous trains, and every train must be a homogeneous consist of
original Arrow cars. The limit is a ride-local contract; it does not provide
arbitrary junctions, mixed-family consists, automatic depots or a park-wide
dispatcher.

## Build or open the example

Run `/fd demo arrow` from the operator position to build the bounded example. The
installed command configures the resulting eight connected Arrow segments as
`arrow_fixed_circuit_v1` automatically. Keep the circuit
inside the normal world and track limits, leave the six block boundaries and
station approach clear, and use the existing Arrow spacing and profile rules.
The example must report the circuit identifier `arrow_fixed_circuit_v1` before
trains are admitted. An example that reports a different circuit is not a 1.2
multi-train fixture.

When driving the private client helper, its `input key slash` action already
opens a command with the leading `/`; type `fd demo arrow` after that action so
the submitted command has exactly one prefix.

The operator panel is the authoritative readback. Open it from the selected Arrow
car's Tools entry. It samples the complete linked consist on the server and shows
the train count, occupied seats, restraints, capture/retention, phase, controller,
power, reservation/occupancy state, fault and any dispatch denial. A client-side
button or stale panel row never grants admission or dispatch.

For the declared route, the panel exposes `route=arrow_fixed_circuit_v1`,
`route_trains`, `route_block`, `route_denial` and `route_managed`. **Admit route
train** sends the guarded action 94 after the complete consist is ready. **Resume
route** sends action 95 after a restart or interruption has been inspected. Both
actions are server-authoritative and remain disabled for an unrelated Arrow ride.

For an authoritative audit of all trains rather than only the selected vehicle,
operators with the validation flag may run `/fd validation route_state`. The
read-only output lists each train's chassis and cars, current and next block,
braking/unresolved/restored flags, and every block reservation owner.

## Admit and dispatch trains

Prepare each train at the station as a complete consist. Board normally, close all
restraints, capture/retain every car and wait for the panel to report the complete
train ready. Admission is whole-consist: every chassis, carrier and native body
must be present and the train must match the original Arrow family contract.

The circuit has six blocks. A moving train reserves its current block and its
next block. The first train may dispatch when those reservations and the station
authority are clear. A second or third train may enter only after its complete
consist is admitted and its requested current/next block pair is free. The third
train is the finite limit; a fourth admission is denied even when the circuit is
otherwise empty.

Dispatch and braking are separate operations. Use the station/controller's
explicit Dispatch action only after the panel says the train is ready. To stop a
train, use the explicit braking/capture action; do not treat a released reservation
or an empty client row as proof that braking completed. The panel must show the
updated phase and reservation state before another admission is attempted.

When admission or dispatch is denied, read the server reason. Typical causes are
an occupied or reserved next block, an incomplete consist, open or mixed restraints,
a missing native body/link, an unavailable controller, a train outside its bound
station, or an unresolved state restored from an interruption. Correct the named
condition and request a fresh panel state. Do not repeat a stale command.

## Removal, unload and recovery

Stop and capture a train before unloading or removing it. The server releases its
current and next block reservations only after the complete consist is stopped,
its riders have left normally, and the train is no longer bound to an active
authority. The panel should then show no unresolved occupancy for those blocks.

If a car, carrier, native body or link is missing, admission and dispatch remain
denied. A chunk unload or restart that restores an incomplete train is fail-closed;
it does not silently rebuild a reservation or allow another train into an unknown
block. Load the affected chunks, inspect the complete consist and use the explicit
resume/recovery action supplied by the current panel. Recheck the circuit and
reservation readback before dispatching again.

The same explicit recovery path applies after an interrupted stop. Recovery must
restore the train identity, occupancy and reservation state before motion resumes;
it is not a second dispatcher and does not increase the three-train limit.

## Player checklist

1. Run `/fd demo arrow`; confirm `route=arrow_fixed_circuit_v1` and the six-block layout.
2. Prepare a complete original Arrow train; board, close restraints and retain.
3. Read `route_trains`, `route_block`, `route_denial` and `route_managed`; use
   **Admit route train** when the complete consist is ready.
4. Dispatch only when the station authority and current/next blocks are clear.
5. Repeat for trains two and three; expect a fourth train to be denied.
6. Brake/capture, unload and verify reservations are released before removal.
7. After interruption or restart, resolve the named missing-body/link/occupancy
   denial and use **Resume route** (action 95) before resuming.

The accepted native and occupied checks belong to FD1701C. This guide supplies the
normal player workflow and diagnostic vocabulary for the transferred FD1701B
controls/example contribution; it does not replace native reservation evidence.
